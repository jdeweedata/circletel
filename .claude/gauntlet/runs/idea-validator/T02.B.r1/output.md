**Quick Verdict: Maybe — the business works, your numbers don't.**

**Why**: Mobile dog grooming is one of the genuinely better small service businesses — recurring revenue on a 6-8 week cycle, customers who don't shop around once they trust you, and a real convenience premium you can charge for. But your plan has two errors pointing the same direction: **6 grooms a day is a salon number, not a van number**, and **£45 is a salon price, not a mobile price**. You've built a forecast where you work the hardest possible days for the least possible money. Fix the price and the plan gets better while you work *less*.

### The one line that matters

**Charging £60 instead of £45 while doing 22 grooms a week earns you the same as doing 30 grooms a week at £45.** Eight fewer dogs. Roughly 16 fewer hours of labour. Less fuel, less water, less wear on the van and on your wrists and shoulders — and the same money in your pocket.

Underpricing is the expensive mistake here, not the van.

## Why 6 a day won't happen

This is the load-bearing assumption in your plan and I think it's wrong for structural reasons, not effort reasons.

- **A full groom on a typical Bristol dog** — cockapoo, spaniel, schnauzer, doodle — is 2 to 2.5 hours in a van. Small smooth-coated dogs are 60-75 minutes. Your average will land near 2 hours.
- **Salon groomers hit 6-8 a day because they overlap.** Dog A kennel-dries while they start clipping Dog B. **In a van you are strictly serial** — one dog, one table, one pair of hands. That's the whole reason mobile does fewer dogs, and it's also the reason mobile charges more.
- **Add travel and setup.** Five hops of 15-20 minutes plus setup/teardown is another 1.5-2 hours.
- 6 grooms × 2hr + 2hr driving = a **14-hour day, five days running**. You'll do it for a fortnight and then you'll be doing four.
- **Water is a hard ceiling.** A hydrobath uses roughly 40-60L per dog. A typical 150L onboard tank is **three dogs before a refill run**. Dog four onwards requires either a refill stop or a client willing to let you run a hose — which some will and many won't.

An experienced mobile groomer with a mature book averages 4-5 a day. Six is a good day of small easy dogs, not a five-day average. Plan on 4.5 and be pleased when it's better.

## Bristol-specific things that will cost you money

**1. The Clean Air Zone.** Bristol's CAZ has been live since late 2022 and covers the city centre. A non-compliant diesel van (pre-Euro 6) is charged around £9 a day. If you're working Clifton, Redland, Southville and crossing the centre, that's **£9 × ~200 days = £1,800 a year — 40 grooms of profit, gone.** Plenty of secondhand conversions in your £22k bracket are older diesels. **Run the reg through the government CAZ vehicle checker before you view any van.** This is the single most checkable, most expensive mistake available to you in this plan.

**2. Residents' parking zones.** Kingsdown, Cotham, Montpelier, Clifton, Southville, Bedminster and more. A van parked two hours in an RPZ without a visitor permit is a ticket, repeatedly. Practically this pushes you toward houses with driveways: **Henleaze, Westbury-on-Trym, Stoke Bishop, Sneyd Park, Long Ashton, Failand, Almondsbury, Backwell.**

That's actually good news, and it's the strategic heart of this business: **the postcodes where you can physically park are the same postcodes that will pay £70 without blinking.** Your parking constraint and your pricing power point at exactly the same map. Stop trying to serve terraced central Bristol at £45 and go serve driveways at £65-75.

**3. Hills.** Clifton, Totterdown, Windmill Hill, Bishopston. A laden van carrying 150L of water (that's 150kg) plus a generator eats fuel and clutches faster than your spreadsheet assumes.

## The three questions your plan doesn't answer

**1. Are you already a qualified, *fast* groomer?** If not, this is the whole answer and the rest is moot. A City & Guilds or iPET Network Level 3 diploma is roughly £3,000-5,000 and 6-12 months. More importantly, **speed takes 2-3 years.** A newly qualified groomer does three dogs a day and is frightened for six hours. Your plan has no line item for the operator's competence, which suggests either you already have it (fine, say so) or you haven't priced it in (not fine).

**2. Where do 240 clients come from?** 30 grooms a week on a 7-week rebook cycle means **roughly 210-240 active recurring clients**. That is the actual asset in this business — not the van. Building that from zero in Bristol is 12-18 months and £3,000-8,000 in marketing. The van is the cheap part; the book is the expensive part, and you've budgeted for the wrong one.

**3. What happens when the van breaks?** You are a single point of failure with a second single point of failure bolted to it. Van off the road = 100% of revenue stops. No sick pay, no holiday pay. I've used 46 working weeks rather than your 48 for exactly this reason, and I'd want 3 months of costs in reserve on top of the £22k before I started.

## Similar Existing Products

*(Verify all of these locally — see the validation exercise below.)*

- **The Groom Room (Pets at Home)** — in-store grooming across the UK including Bristol sites, typically around £30-45 for a full groom. It is effectively a loss-leader supporting a retail estate, so it can undercut you permanently. **This is the "market leader is entrenched and cheap" flag — and it's exactly why competing at £45 is a losing position.** You cannot out-price them and you shouldn't try. You beat them on convenience, on one-dog-at-a-time low-stress handling, and on the same groomer every time.
- **Established Bristol mobile operators** — the city already has several. This market is not empty, which is good news: it proves demand and it gives you a price ceiling to read. Expect to find full grooms in the **£50-£85** range depending on size and coat, which tells you your £45 is roughly 30% under market.
- **Fixed salons across Bedminster, Bishopston, Fishponds, Gloucester Road** — £40-65, higher throughput than you can achieve, but the customer has to drive there, park, and come back. Your entire premium is that they don't.
- **Marketplace platforms (Tailster, Rover-adjacent listings, Bark)** — lead generation that takes a cut and puts you in a price race with the cheapest person on the list. Fine for the first 20 clients to fill a diary; a bad primary channel to build a business on.

**Your real competitive position**: mobile is genuinely differentiated for elderly owners, reactive or anxious dogs, multi-dog households, and people without cars. Those people are not price-shopping. **Charging £45 signals to them that you're the budget option, which is the opposite of what you are.**

## What Would Make This Stronger

1. **Reprice to £60-70 before you do anything else.** Test it in an afternoon: ring 10 Bristol mobile groomers as a customer with a cockapoo and ask their price and their next available slot. If they're at £65 with a three-week wait, your £45 is leaving £15,000 a year on the table for no extra work. Price by size and coat condition, and add a matted-coat surcharge in writing so you're never doing three hours of dematting for a £45 fee.

2. **Don't buy the van first — build the book first.** This is the highest-value change on the list. Work Saturdays for an existing mobile groomer, or do home visits with hand tools and no bath, and **get 40-60 paying clients before £22,000 leaves your account.** If you can't fill three days of a diary, you don't need a van, you need customers — and you'll have learned that for £0 instead of £22,000. If you *can* fill three days, you'll buy the van with a waiting list already attached and hit profitability in month two instead of month fourteen.

3. **Only look at Euro 6 vans, and check the reg against the CAZ checker before every viewing.** Non-negotiable. £1,800/year is not a rounding error at this scale.

4. **Rebook in the van, before you take payment.** This is the entire difference between a good grooming round and a bad one. Groomers who book the next appointment at the door run 85%+ diary fill; ones who say "I'll text you in eight weeks" run about 50%. Everything in your forecast depends on utilisation, and this one habit is the biggest lever on it.

5. **Add the high-margin, low-water services.** Nail trims at £12-15 for 15 minutes with no bath and no dryer. De-shed treatments at +£15. Puppy introduction grooms as a deliberate loss-leader — a 12-week-old cockapoo groomed gently is a client for 13 years. These fill the gaps a full-groom-only diary can't.

6. **Get insurance with care, custody and control cover before dog number one.** Standard public liability doesn't cover the animal in your care. Eventually you will nick an ear, and heat incidents under dryers do happen. This is the risk that ends businesses.

7. **Consider buying a retiring groomer's client book.** Groomers retire constantly and a warm list of 150 recurring clients is often available for a few thousand pounds. That's cheaper than the marketing spend to build the same book and it arrives instantly. Ask around the Bristol grooming groups.

## Do this before you spend anything

An afternoon of work that answers the question properly:

- Ring 10 Bristol mobile groomers as a customer. Log price, size bands, and next available slot. **Their waiting times are your demand signal** — long waits mean the market is under-served and your price should be higher.
- Post in 3-4 Bristol area Facebook groups (Henleaze, Westbury, Long Ashton, Bishopston) offering grooming at £65 and count the replies. Costs nothing. If ten people ask for a slot, you have a business. If two do, you've saved yourself £22,000.
- Run three candidate van regs through the CAZ checker.
- Get one real insurance quote with care, custody and control included.

## Bottom line

The model is sound — recurring, defensible, real demand, and mobile carries a genuine premium. **The plan is wrong in a fixable way**: you've priced yourself as the cheap option in a market where you're structurally the premium one, and then tried to make the numbers work with volume your body and your water tank can't deliver.

At your stated numbers you'd take home about £33,000 — but you won't hit those numbers. At realistic volume and your £45 price, you take home about **£21,000 for 45-50 hours a week of physical labour, having put £22,000 of your own capital at risk.** That's roughly what a salon would pay you as an employee, with none of the risk and none of the capital. **That version isn't worth it.**

At realistic volume and a correct £60-65 price, you take home around **£32,000 with a saner working week and a payback on the van of about two years including ramp-up.** That version is worth it.

Same van, same kit, same you. The £15 is the whole business.
