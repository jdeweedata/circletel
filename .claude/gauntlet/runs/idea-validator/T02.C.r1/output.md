**Quick Verdict: Maybe — conditional on one thing you didn't mention**

**Why**: The business model is sound — mobile dog grooming is a proven, low-break-even, recurring-revenue micro-business, and almost none of the usual killers apply (no network effect, no behaviour change, no enterprise sales, customers already pay for this). But your two headline numbers are both wrong in the same direction: £45 is under-priced for Bristol mobile, and 6 grooms a day is a fantasy you'll hold for about three weeks before your shoulders quit. The condition: you never said whether you can actually groom. If you're already a qualified/experienced groomer, this is a Build-it. If you're buying a £22k van to learn the trade, it's a Skip-it — you're funding an apprenticeship with a depreciating asset.

## The arithmetic (this is the actual answer)

**Your plan as stated:** 6 × £45 × 5 days × 48 weeks = £64,800 revenue. Looks great. That's why it's suspicious.

**Why 6/day doesn't survive contact with Bristol:**
- A proper full groom (bath, high-velocity dry, clip, scissor, nails, ears, anal glands) is 1.5–2 hrs on an average dog. Six = 9–12 hrs of grooming.
- Add drive time between clients: 15–30 min each in Bristol, more if you're crossing the city. That's another 1.5–2.5 hrs.
- Add tank fill, generator, setup/teardown, payment, notes, rebooking.
- You've built a 12–13 hour day. Sustainable output for mobile is **3–5 dogs/day**; 4–4.5 is the honest planning number. Six is achievable only on a day of short-coat bath-and-tidies in one postcode.

**Why £45 is the bigger mistake:** mobile is a *premium* format — one dog at a time, no cage drying, no all-day stay, door-to-door. In Bristol that should command 20–40% over salon. £45 prices you against The Groom Room, which is a fight you lose on cost structure. You are leaving £10–20 per dog on the table, roughly £15–20k a year.

**Realistic cost base (annual, 46 working weeks):**

*Fixed ~£8,000–11,000:* van depreciation £2,800 if cash (or ~£5,700/yr if financed at ~11% over 5 yrs) · insurance, commercial vehicle + £2m public liability + care/custody/control £1,300–2,000 · servicing/MOT/tyres/brakes on a heavy stop-start van £1,200–1,800 · breakdown cover £200 · accountant £600 · booking software £300–450 · phone/website/Google £400 · marketing and lead-gen £800–1,500 · CPD/canine first aid £200.

*Variable ~£8–10 per dog:* shampoo/conditioner/cologne £1.50–2.50 · blade sharpening and replacement £1.50–2 (blades dull fast and are £25–30 each) · towel laundry £0.50 · water + generator fuel £1–1.50 · card fees £0.50–1 · diesel £2.50–3.50 (50–70 miles/day at ~£0.22/mile).

**Three scenarios, 4.5 dogs/day, 5 days, 46 weeks = 1,035 grooms:**

| | £45 | £55 | £60 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | £46,575 | £56,925 | £62,100 |
| Variable @£9 | (£9,315) | (£9,315) | (£9,315) |
| Fixed | (£9,500) | (£9,500) | (£9,500) |
| **Net profit** | **£27,760** | **£38,110** | **£43,285** |
| Take-home after tax/NI | ~£23,600 | ~£31,500 | ~£35,300 |

**The punchline:** your 6-a-day-at-£45 plan nets ~£41,800 before tax. **4.5 a day at £60 nets ~£43,300** — more money, 25% fewer dogs, three fewer hours a day, and a back that still works at 50. Raising your price by £15 beats working a third harder. If you take one thing from this, take that.

**Year 1 is the one that hurts.** You will not have a full book. Realistically ~3/day average across year 1 = ~690 grooms × £50 = £34,500, less ~£15,700 costs = **~£19k profit**. That's sub-minimum-wage for the hours. Budget for it or it will break you.

**The good news on capital:** break-even is only ~207 grooms/year (~4.5 dogs a *week*) to cover fixed costs. And you need only ~160 loyal dogs on a 7-week rebook cycle to fill 4.5/day. Against an employed alternative, the £22k pays back in under two years. That's genuinely decent capital efficiency — better than most software ideas I look at.

**Similar Existing Products**:
- **The Groom Room (Pets at Home)**: national in-store salons, several around Bristol (Cribbs, Longwell Green, Imperial, Avonmeads). Key differentiator: scale, footfall and low headline prices — they are your price anchor and you must not compete with them on price. Beat them on convenience and single-dog handling instead.
- **Dial a Dog Wash**: established UK mobile grooming franchise. Key differentiator: brand, van spec and a proven ops playbook — bought with a franchise fee and ongoing royalties. Useful as proof the mobile model works; also proof you can do it independently for less.
- **Independent solo mobile groomers** (Facebook, Nextdoor, local groups): your real competitive set — 5 to 15 of them working Bristol and the fringe. Key differentiator: none, mostly. They compete on price and availability, which is exactly the gap for someone with a professional booking system, deposits and reliable timekeeping.
- **Independent high-street salons** (Bedminster, Gloucester Rd, Fishponds, Kingswood): key differentiator: cheaper per groom, but require drop-off, waiting and cage time — which is the pain you're selling against.
- **Bark.com / Tailster and similar lead platforms**: will happily sell you leads. Key differentiator: they arbitrage groomers against each other on price. Use for the first 20 clients only, then get off them.

*Honest caveat: I can't verify live Bristol operator names or current prices right now. Before you spend a penny, mystery-shop it — phone or WhatsApp 10 local groomers as a customer with a specific dog ("cockapoo, full groom, BS9") and write down every quote. Two hours of work, and it settles the pricing question with real data instead of my estimate.*

**What Would Make This Stronger**:

- **Reprice before you buy the van.** Kill the flat £45. Band it: small/short coat £48–52, medium £58–65, large/double-coat £70–85, doodle/poodle-cross premium, de-matting surcharge charged by the 15 minutes, and a £10–15 travel band outside your core radius. Publish the bands so you never negotiate on the doorstep.
- **Test the trade before the capital.** If you're not already grooming full-time, do 20–30 grooms first — Saturdays for an established salon, or rent a table. You will learn whether you can physically do four a day at quality, which is the single variable the entire £22k rests on. If you *are* an experienced groomer with clients who'd follow you, say so and this moves straight to Build it.
- **Buy the conversion, not the van.** A £22k secondhand grooming van can be a bargain or a rolling bill. Get the *conversion* inspected, not just the chassis: generator hours, hot-water system, plumbing, damp and rot behind the bath (wet vans rot), electrics, table hydraulics, dryer condition. And confirm the base van is **Euro 6** — Bristol's Clean Air Zone charges £9/day for non-compliant vans, and the CAZ sits directly over Clifton, Cotham, Redland and the centre. Non-compliant costs you £2k+/yr or locks you out of the postcodes with the money. Hold £3k contingency on top of the £22k.
- **Engineer route density from day one.** Assign postcode clusters to fixed days (e.g. Mon Henleaze/Westbury/Stoke Bishop, Tue Portishead/Clevedon, Wed Long Ashton/Nailsea/Chew Valley…). Never criss-cross the city. Density is the difference between 3/day and 5/day at the same effort. Bonus: suburbs and villages have **driveways** — inner Bristol has residents' parking zones and no legal spot to sit a 3.5t van for 90 minutes, which is a daily headache in exactly the areas that look most attractive on paper.
- **Rebook on the doorstep, every single time.** Your asset is not the van, it's a book of ~160–200 dogs on a 6–8 week cycle. Take a card deposit at booking and enforce a 48-hour cancellation policy from client #1 — a mobile no-show costs you the slot *and* the drive, and you can't backfill it.
- **Cover the single-point-of-failure risks.** You are the business: no groom, no income. Get income protection (~£30–50/mo), breakdown cover, a written matting/aggressive-dog refusal policy (you're alone in a van with no second pair of hands — salons have help, you don't), and dispose of waste water legally, never down a storm drain.

---

**Two red flags worth naming explicitly:**

1. **"This is a job, not a business."** There's no leverage — revenue is capped by your hands, and it stops the day you stop. That's fine if you want a ~£35k self-employed trade with autonomy; it's not fine if you're expecting an asset that runs without you. And note the trap in scaling: a second van and an employed groomer pushes you toward the £90k VAT threshold with almost no input VAT to reclaim on labour — a 20% haircut that eats the second van's margin. One-van-priced-properly usually beats two-vans-priced-cheaply.

2. **Grooming itself is not licensable in England** under the 2018 animal activities regulations (boarding, day care, breeding and selling are) — so no council licence for grooming alone, but confirm with Bristol City Council and check your home lease/deeds and any residents' parking restrictions before you park a liveried commercial van outside your house.

**Bottom line:** the idea is fine — the plan is mispriced and over-scheduled. Fix the price to £55–65, plan for 4–4.5 dogs a day, budget a lean year one, and only buy the van if you can already groom. Do those four things and the £22k is a reasonable bet. Do it as written, at £45 and six a day, and you'll be exhausted, under-earning and quietly resentful by Christmas.
